# What RescueConsole does, workspace by workspace

Eight workspaces that share one record of every animal and every person, and the forms, workflows, website and settings that sit across all of them.

Every screenshot on this page is the product as it runs today, captured against a demonstration organization. Nothing is a mock-up.

Animals

## Every animal, from the day it arrives to the day it leaves

One record per animal that holds everything: where it is, what it needs, who is looking after it, who has applied for it, and what it has been through.

- Your own status vocabulary, in groups you name, each with a color, a public name and a care state that drives the counts on the dashboard.
- Intake, weights, litters and bonded pairs, photos with a chosen profile shot, notes and a full history, with a working name inside and a plain one on your website.
- A real medical record: vaccines and treatments with due dates, physical exams, procedures and surgeries, diagnoses and diagnostic tests, the spay/neuter summary, and a medication list you keep in Settings. A scheduled vet visit is an appointment on the calendar, and finishing it asks what happened.
- A placement card (children and how young, other dogs and cats, a fenced yard, hours alone) checked against every application for that animal.
- Applications, adoptions with fees and receipts, foster homes with capacity, transports as relay legs, and printable cage cards and flyers.
- Lists that filter on any field, save as views, select in bulk, and export to CSV with the fields you can see or all of them.

People

## One person, however many roles they hold

Somebody can be a volunteer, a foster and a donor at once. RescueConsole keeps them as one record, so their hours, their placements and their gifts sit side by side.

- Roles rather than record types: add a role, keep the history. A tab per role shows what that person is to you.
- Structured names and addresses with country-aware labels, several phone numbers and email addresses, and optional address verification.
- Public forms become people. An adoption, foster or volunteer application turns into a person record with one click, or links to the one you already have.
- Duplicate detection, import from your existing spreadsheet, and export of every field including the custom ones you added.

Shifts and volunteers

## Volunteers book themselves in. You set the rules.

Open a window, say which roles it needs and how many, and volunteers take the hours they can do. Rules about notice, hours per day and where somebody is approved to work are enforced for you.

- Partial or whole-shift booking with minimum and maximum lengths, so a six-hour Saturday can be covered by three people.
- Roles and locations volunteers must be approved for before they can book, with expiring qualifications.
- A week and day grid that shows overlap, plus month and agenda views, and a separate calendar for everything dated across the whole organization.
- Cancellation requests go to a coordinator when inside the notice window, and every request is kept, so the queue says "asked twice, refused once".
- Sign-off locks hours for grant reports, and a Volunteers page shows who is still waiting to be approved and how long they have waited.

Communications

## A shared inbox that knows who is writing

Publish an address per team (adoptions, fosters, the volunteer coordinator), and every reply threads back into the conversation it belongs to, next to the person and animal it is about.

- As many inboxes as you need, each with its own access list, so the adoptions team does not read the treasurer’s mail.
- Replies in rich text with templates and a personal signature; open and click tracking on what you send.
- Send as noreply on our domain, or verify your own and send as yourself. Inbound mail arrives by forwarding the address you already publish.
- Every message is filed against the records it concerns, and an audit log for administrators records who sent what.

Fundraising

## Every gift on the donor’s record, and reconciled to the bank

Log one-off, recurring and post-adoption gifts against campaigns, see lifetime and lapsed giving per donor, and match the payout your processor sends against what you recorded.

- Take gifts by card on your own site through your own Stripe account, and send adoption-fee links that offer a gift on top. You choose who pays the processing fee.
- Campaigns with goals and progress, donors as a first-class list, and a wish list for the things you need in kind.
- Reconciliation against Stripe, Square, Zeffy and PayPal payout files, with every unmatched line called out.
- Donation amounts and adoption fees are yours to set, by species and age group, with receipts that carry your legal name and tax status.

Events

## The adoption day, the clinic and the gala, with the people who staff them

An event is the occasion; a shift is the work. Events link to the shifts that cover them and the animals who will attend, and appear on your website the moment you publish them.

- Adoption days, fundraisers, clinics, training and meetings, each with a status and a public toggle.
- Coverage at a glance: which events still have places to fill.
- Published events go straight to your public site with the animals attending.

Organizations

## The vet, the pet store and the partner rescue, and who works there

A vet who moves practices does not rewrite the medical records they signed. Affiliations are dated, so history stays true.

- Veterinary practices, rescues, shelters, suppliers and employers, with a primary practice and an emergency practice you can pick on a medical record.
- Relationship and industry on every organization (prospect, client, partner, supplier), kept separately from what the organization is.
- Your own organization has a page too: locations, hours and the address that appears on your site and your flyers.

Public Registry

## What one rescue learned, every rescue can check

A shared, cross-organization registry of placement risks and registered microchips. You flag; two other rescues corroborate; identifiers are hashed so a search matches without exposing anybody’s details.

- Flags for animal abuse, neglect, fraud and abandonment with a narrative, a severity and an evidence trail.
- Searches match on hashed email, phone and microchip; only the narrative, a name and a city are shown to another rescue.
- Your own animals’ microchips can be published so a found animal reaches you, with a contact block that is separate from your public details.
- Anybody who finds an animal can look a chip up on our public site without an account, and reach the rescue that registered it. No sign-in, and nothing shown but how to make contact.

Calendar, tasks and documents

## The things that are dated, the things that are due, and the paperwork

- One calendar for everything with a date across every workspace (shifts, schedules, events, medical due dates, arrivals, adoptions and tasks), narrowed to what you may see.
- Tasks from any record, with due dates, priorities and an assignee, on the dashboard agenda and on the record they belong to.
- Documents against any record or the organization as a whole, with categories and expiry dates, so a lapsed insurance certificate is something the page tells you about.

Across every workspace

## What is true everywhere

### Your public website, from your data

Eight standard pages (adopt, donate, volunteer, foster, about, resources, events, contact), filled from what you already record, on your own domain, with your logo, your accent color and your forms. Or embed the animal grid in the site you have.

contoso.rescueconsole.com/app/settings/public

![The Public page settings: how an animal becomes public, what is showing right now, and the snippet that puts the animals on your own website.](https://rescueconsole.com/_astro/public-page.yF-_qr2Z_18VxFW.webp)

### Forms and workflows, not paperwork

Build the adoption, foster, volunteer, surrender and donor forms your organization uses, with your own fields and sections. Submissions land in one inbox. Workflows turn a process like intake into a checklist that runs on the record, creates the tasks, and can change a status or send a template when a step completes.

### The record, arranged your way

Custom fields on people, animals, organizations, donations, campaigns, shifts and events. Record layouts decide which fields each form shows, in what order, and which are required, including a tab of fields per role a person holds.

### Statuses that mean something

Eighteen shelter statuses in three groups to start, each with a color, a purpose and a care state. Rename them, regroup them, add your own. The care state is what drives occupancy, the dashboard and what the public sees.

### Volunteers, from sign-up to first shift

A volunteer who signs in sees three things: their shifts, the shifts they can take, and the events. Coordinators see who is waiting for approval and how long they have waited, who is approved and has not started, and who is taking shifts.

### Paperwork, written once and filed where it belongs

Adoption contracts and agreements written the way you write an email, filled from the record so nobody retypes a name, and signed. Upload the paperwork you already have, like a vet invoice, a surrender form or a photo of a signed page, against the animal or the person it belongs to. Print an animal’s record on your own letterhead when somebody needs it on paper.

### Roles, permissions and an audit trail

Thirteen built-in roles, from organization admin to read only, with the vets, counselors and coordinators between, plus your own with per-workspace read, write and admin. Every change to every record is logged with what it was before and after, so a mistake can be put back by hand.

### Your data is yours

Import animals, people, donations and events from a spreadsheet with column mapping. Export every list, with custom fields, as CSV. API keys with the same permissions as a member, and webhooks that tell Zapier, Make or your own system about every change.

### We build what rescues ask for, and we show our working

There is a board inside the app of what other rescues have asked for. You can vote on something instead of writing it again, and the roadmap on this site is written from that board, so what we are going to build next is the list you voted on, not a list we made up. What has already landed is in the changelog, dated.

### Built for the way rescues actually work

Time zones on the organization and on the person, so a Saturday shift is Saturday for everybody. Structured addresses with country-aware labels. A volunteer who signs in sees their shifts and nothing they cannot open.
